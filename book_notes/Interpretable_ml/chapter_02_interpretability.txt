## 🎯 Teach It Summary

Interpretability is the ability for humans to understand why a machine learning model made a certain decision. We often need this because correct predictions alone don't solve the full problem—especially when fairness, safety, or trust is involved. Models can be biased, learn spurious correlations (like wolves vs snow), or make decisions that impact people's lives. But interpretability isn't always needed: low-risk applications or well-studied systems don't require explanations, and sometimes explanations let users game the system.

The most important insight is what humans actually want from explanations: we don't want the complete truth, we want short, contrastive answers—"Why THIS instead of THAT?"—that focus on unusual causes. For example, when a loan is rejected, we want to know what would need to change to get approval, not an exhaustive list of all factors. The explanation should be selective (1-3 causes), socially appropriate to the audience, and highlight abnormal conditions. While fidelity (truthfulness) matters, selectivity, contrast, and social context are actually more important. This means ML systems need to be designed with human psychology in mind, not just accuracy.

## 📝 Personal Takeaways

1. **Interpretability is about humans, not models** — it's a human-centered concept, not a mathematical property

2. **The "why" matters as much as the "what"** — correct predictions alone are often incomplete solutions

3. **Good explanations are psychologically grounded** — they match how humans naturally think (contrastive, selective, abnormal-focused)

4. **There's tension between truth and usability** — selective explanations are inherently incomplete, but that's what humans prefer

5. **Not every problem needs interpretability** — sometimes it's unnecessary or even counterproductive

6. **The social context is crucial** — the same model needs different explanations for different audiences

7. **Interpretability is a tool, not a goal** — it serves purposes like fairness, safety, trust, and learning

8. **Be careful with proxy features** — they make systems gameable and can lead to spurious correlations

9. **The trade-off is nuanced** — interpretability doesn't always mean sacrificing performance

10. **This chapter sets up the WHY** — later chapters will cover the HOW (specific methods and techniques)
