### Decision Trees

<details>
<summary><b>Decision Tree</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A hierarchical data structure that implements a divide-and-conquer strategy with nodes as feature tests and leaves as category labels.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>ID3 Algorithm</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A recursive greedy algorithm (Quinlan, 1970s) that builds trees by selecting the attribute with highest information gain at each step; finding the minimal tree is NP-hard.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Entropy</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `H(S) = −p₊log₂(p₊) − p₋log₂(p₋)` — measures impurity; 0 when all examples same class, 1 when perfectly balanced (maximum uncertainty).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Information Gain</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `Gain(S,A) = H(S) − Σᵥ (|Sᵥ|/|S|) × H(Sᵥ)` — measures reduction in entropy after splitting on attribute A; higher gain = better split.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Overfitting in Decision Trees</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** When a tree has smaller error on training data but larger error on test data due to noise or too much variance; addressed by pre-pruning (stop early) or post-pruning (remove leaves).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Pruning</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Removing leaves and assigning the majority label of the parent; evaluated using cross-validation, statistical testing, or minimum description length.

</details>

---

### Bagging & Random Forests

<details>
<summary><b>Bagging (Bootstrap Aggregating)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Creates B bootstrap samples (sampling with replacement, ~63% of data per sample), fits a model to each, and averages predictions — reduces variance without changing bias.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Out-of-Bag (OOB) Error</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The ~37% of observations not in each bootstrap sample are used as validation; for large B, OOB error is virtually equivalent to LOOCV, eliminating the need for a separate validation set.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Random Forests</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Bagging with trees PLUS at each split, consider a random subset of m < p predictors (default m ≈ √p for classification), producing less correlated trees and better generalization.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Bagging vs Random Forests</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Bagging considers all p predictors at each split (high correlation, higher test error); Random Forests considers m < p predictors (low correlation, lower test error).

</details>

---

### Boosting

<details>
<summary><b>Boosting</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Sequentially combines weak learners, each trying to improve upon its predecessor by re-weighting misclassified examples; reduces bias but can overfit.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Boosting vs Bagging</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Boosting is sequential (high dependency, reduces bias, can overfit); Bagging is parallel (low dependency, reduces variance, can't overfit).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDT)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Additive model where new trees are added sequentially: `ŷᵢ⁽ᵗ⁾ = ŷᵢ⁽ᵗ⁻¹⁾ + fₜ(xᵢ)`, with objective `Obj = Σ l(yᵢ, ŷᵢ) + Σ ω(fₜ)` where ω penalizes tree complexity.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Taylor Expansion in GBDT</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Approximates the objective using first-order (gᵢ) and second-order (hᵢ) gradients, allowing direct optimization of any loss function by computing only g and h.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Tree Definition in GBDT</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Each leaf j has weight wⱼ (prediction), complexity `ω(f) = γT + ½λΣ wⱼ²`, and optimal leaf weight `wⱼ* = −Gⱼ/(Hⱼ+λ)`.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Gain Formula for Splitting</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `Gain = ½[G_L²/(H_L+λ) + G_R²/(H_R+λ) − (G_L+G_R)²/(H_L+H_R+λ)] − γ` — splits are chosen if gain is positive.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Pre-stopping vs Post-pruning</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Pre-stopping stops splits with negative gain (risks missing beneficial future splits); post-pruning grows full tree then prunes leaf splits with negative gain (generally better).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>XGBoost</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** eXtreme Gradient Boosting — optimized GBDT with parallel tree construction, distributed computing, out-of-core computing, sparsity-awareness, shrinkage, and column subsampling.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>XGBoost System Optimizations</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Block structure for linear scan sorting, cache-aware prefetching to reduce cache misses, and out-of-core computing for datasets larger than memory.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>XGBoost Algorithm Features</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Regularized objective (penalizes leaves/weights), shrinkage (learning rate), column subsampling, sparsity-awareness for missing values, and approximate split finding.

</details>

### GBDT Formulas

<details>
<summary><b>GBDT Core Formulas</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Formula | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| `ŷᵢ⁽ᵗ⁾ = ŷᵢ⁽ᵗ⁻¹⁾ + fₜ(xᵢ)` | Additive model update |
| `Obj = Σ l(yᵢ, ŷᵢ) + Σ ω(fₜ)` | GBDT objective |
| `ω(f) = γT + ½λΣ wⱼ²` | Tree complexity |
| `wⱼ* = −Gⱼ/(Hⱼ+λ)` | Optimal leaf weight |
| `Score = −½Σ [Gⱼ²/(Hⱼ+λ)] + γT` | Tree score |

</details>

### Split Gain Formula

<details>
<summary><b>Gain Formula</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

**Formula:** `Gain = ½[G_L²/(H_L+λ) + G_R²/(H_R+λ) − (G_L+G_R)²/(H_L+H_R+λ)] − γ`

**Interpretation:** Split is accepted if Gain > 0.

</details>
