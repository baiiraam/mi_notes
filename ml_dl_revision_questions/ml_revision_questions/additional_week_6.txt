### Dimensionality Reduction

<details>
<summary><b>Dimensionality Reduction</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Mapping data to a lower-dimensional space while preserving important structure — reduces overfitting, saves computation, mitigates the curse of dimensionality, and enables visualization.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Principal Component Analysis (PCA)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A linear method that finds the directions of maximum variance (principal components — top eigenvectors of the covariance matrix), projecting data to a lower-dimensional subspace.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>PCA — Two Equivalent Criteria</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Minimize reconstruction error OR maximize projected variance — they are equivalent because `projected variance + reconstruction error = total variance` (Pythagorean Theorem).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>PCA — Solution</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The principal components are the top K eigenvectors of the empirical covariance matrix `Σ̂ = (1/n)Σ (xᵢ − µ)(xᵢ − µ)ᵀ`; for K=1, it's the direction of maximum variance.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>t-SNE</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding — a non-linear method specialized for visualization that preserves local topology by converting distances to probabilities and minimizing KL divergence.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>t-SNE — Perplexity</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A smooth measure of the effective number of neighbors (typical range 5–50, commonly 30) — controls the balance between local and global structure in the visualization.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>t-SNE — Crowding Problem</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** There is much more space in high dimensions than in low dimensions; t-SNE uses Student-t distribution in low-D (heavier tails) to give more space and separate points in the visualization.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>t-SNE — Cost Function</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `C = Σᵢ KL(Pᵢ || Qᵢ) = Σᵢ Σⱼ pⱼ|ᵢ log(pⱼ|ᵢ / qⱼ|ᵢ)` — sum of KL divergences, optimized with gradient descent + momentum.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>PCA vs t-SNE</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** PCA is linear, preserves global variance, and is fast; t-SNE is non-linear, preserves local topology, is slower, and is specialized for visualization (stochastic runs may differ).

</details>

---

### Density Estimation

<details>
<summary><b>Kernel Density Estimation (KDE)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A non-parametric density estimator that places a kernel function on every data point and averages them: `f̂(x) = (1/(nh)) Σᵢ K((x − xᵢ)/h)`.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Bandwidth (h) in KDE</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Controls smoothness — small h = under-smoothing (high variance, overfitting), large h = over-smoothing (high bias, underfitting); optimal h balances bias and variance.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Silverman's Rule of Thumb</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `h ≈ 1.06 × σ × n⁻¹/⁵` for Gaussian kernel — assumes normal distribution; a good starting point but can fail for multimodal data; ISJ algorithm is more robust.

</details>

---

### Clustering

<details>
<summary><b>Clustering</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** An unsupervised learning method that assigns observations to homogeneous subgroups without class labels; algorithms include K-means, Hierarchical clustering, and GMMs.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>K-Means Clustering</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Minimizes within-cluster variation (sum of squared distances to centroids) via iterative assignment and centroid updates; requires K a-priori and converges to a local minimum.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Hierarchical Clustering</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Produces a dendrogram (tree structure) of nested clusters; agglomerative (bottom-up) is most common — start with each point as its own cluster, iteratively fuse closest clusters.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Linkage Methods</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Define inter-cluster dissimilarity: Complete (maximum distance), Average (mean of all pairwise distances), Single (minimum distance — causes chaining), Centroid (distance between centroids — causes inversions).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Correlation Distance</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Clusters based on patterns/shapes rather than magnitudes — useful for market segmentation where purchase patterns matter more than total volume.

</details>

---

### Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs)

<details>
<summary><b>Generative View of Clustering</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Imagine data is generated by choosing a cluster z with probability πₖ, then sampling x from a Gaussian: `p(x) = Σₖ πₖ N(x|µₖ, Σₖ)` — a Gaussian Mixture Model.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>GMM Parameters</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** πₖ (mixing coefficients — cluster probabilities), µₖ (means), Σₖ (covariances); often assume Σₖ = I for simplicity, but full covariance allows different spatial extents.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Responsibility (GMM)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `rₙₖ = p(zₙ = k | xₙ)` — the probability that cluster k generated data point n; computed using Bayes rule, representing soft assignment in EM algorithm.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>EM Algorithm for GMMs</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Expectation-Maximization alternates between E-step (compute responsibilities given current parameters) and M-step (re-estimate parameters given responsibilities) until convergence.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>GMM — EM M-Step Updates</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `πₖ = (1/n)Σ rₙₖ`, `µₖ = (Σ rₙₖ xₙ)/(Σ rₙₖ)`, `Σₖ = (Σ rₙₖ (xₙ−µₖ)(xₙ−µₖ)ᵀ)/(Σ rₙₖ)` — weighted averages by responsibilities.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>K-means vs GMMs</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** K-means is hard assignment with spherical clusters; GMMs provide soft (probabilistic) assignments and can model clusters with different shapes and variances.

</details>

### PCA — Core Formulas

<details>
<summary><b>PCA Formulas</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Formula | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| `z = Uᵀ(x − µ)` | Code/representation (projection) |
| `x̂ = µ + Uz` | Reconstruction in original space |
| `Σ̂ = (1/n)Σ (xᵢ−µ)(xᵢ−µ)ᵀ` | Empirical covariance matrix |
| `‖x − µ‖² = ‖projection‖² + ‖residual‖²` | Pythagorean Theorem (total variance = projected variance + reconstruction error) |
| Principal components | Top K eigenvectors of Σ̂ |

</details>

---

### PCA — Two Equivalent Criteria

<details>
<summary><b>Reconstruction Error vs Projected Variance</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Criterion | Goal |
|-----------|------|
| **Minimize reconstruction error** | `(1/n)Σᵢ ‖xᵢ − x̂ᵢ‖²` |
| **Maximize projected variance** | `(1/n)Σᵢ ‖zᵢ − mean(z)‖²` |

**They are equivalent!** Maximizing variance ≡ minimizing reconstruction error.

</details>

---

### t-SNE — Key Concepts

<details>
<summary><b>t-SNE Summary</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Purpose** | Visualization (2D/3D) |
| **Preserves** | Local structure / topology |
| **High-D similarity** | Gaussian: `pⱼ|ᵢ ∝ exp(−‖xᵢ−xⱼ‖² / 2σᵢ²)` |
| **Low-D similarity** | Student-t: `qⱼ|ᵢ ∝ (1 + ‖yᵢ−yⱼ‖²)⁻¹` |
| **Cost function** | `KL(P || Q) = Σ pⱼ|ᵢ log(pⱼ|ᵢ / qⱼ|ᵢ)` |
| **Perplexity** | Typical range 5–50 (commonly 30) |

</details>

---

### KDE — Core Formula

<details>
<summary><b>KDE Formula</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Formula | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| `f̂(x) = (1/(nh)) Σᵢ K((x − xᵢ)/h)` | KDE (unweighted) |
| `f̂(x) = (1/(Σwᵢ)) Σᵢ wᵢ × (1/h) K((x − xᵢ)/h)` | KDE (weighted) |
| `h ≈ 1.06 × σ × n⁻¹/⁵` | Silverman's rule of thumb (Gaussian kernel) |

</details>

---

### KDE — Common Kernels

<details>
<summary><b>Kernel Functions</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Kernel | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| **Gaussian** | `K(x) ∝ exp(−x²/2)` (most common) |
| **Triangular** | `K(x) ∝ max(1−|x|, 0)` |
| **Box** | `K(x) ∝ 1` for `|x| ≤ 1`, 0 otherwise |
| **Triweight** | `K(x) ∝ (1−x²)³` for `|x| ≤ 1` |

</details>

### K-Means

<details>
<summary><b>K-Means Summary</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Objective** | Minimize `Σⱼ Σᵢ∈Cⱼ ‖xᵢ − µⱼ‖²` |
| **Algorithm** | (1) Random init, (2) Assign to nearest centroid, (3) Update centroids, repeat |
| **Convergence** | Always to a local minimum |
| **Randomness** | Different initializations → different minima |

</details>

---

### Hierarchical Clustering

<details>
<summary><b>Linkage Methods</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Method | Definition | Issue |
|--------|------------|-------|
| **Complete** | Maximum distance between pairs | - |
| **Average** | Average of all pairwise distances | - |
| **Single** | Minimum distance between pairs | Chaining phenomenon |
| **Centroid** | Distance between centroids | Inversions |

</details>

---

### GMM — EM Algorithm

<details>
<summary><b>GMM EM Steps</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| **E-step** | Compute responsibilities: `rₙₖ = p(zₙ=k|xₙ)` given current π, µ, Σ |
| **M-step** | Re-estimate: `πₖ = (1/n)Σrₙₖ`; `µₖ = (Σrₙₖxₙ)/(Σrₙₖ)`; `Σₖ = (Σrₙₖ(xₙ−µₖ)(xₙ−µₖ)ᵀ)/(Σrₙₖ)` |
| **Convergence** | Log likelihood does not decrease; non-convex (different initializations) |

</details>

---

### GMM — Marginal Distribution

<details>
<summary><b>GMM Formulas</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Formula | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| `p(x) = Σᵢ πᵢ N(x|µᵢ, Σᵢ)` | Marginal distribution (GMM) |
| `rₙₖ = p(zₙ=k|xₙ)` | Responsibility (soft assignment) |
| `log p(X) = Σₙ log [Σₖ πₖ N(xₙ|µₖ, Σₖ)]` | Log likelihood |

</details>

---

### K-means vs GMMs

<details>
<summary><b>K-means vs GMMs</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Aspect | K-means | GMMs |
|--------|---------|------|
| **Assignment** | Hard (one cluster) | Soft (probabilities/responsibilities) |
| **Covariance** | Spherical (implicit) | Can be full covariance |
| **Probabilities** | No | Yes |
| **Type** | Geometric | Probabilistic (generative) |

</details>
