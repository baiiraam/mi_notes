---

<summary><b>Supervised Learning</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Learning from labeled data where the model maps inputs to known outputs (e.g., spam detection, house price prediction).

---

<details>
<summary><b>Unsupervised Learning</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Finding hidden patterns in unlabeled data without predefined outputs (e.g., customer segmentation, topic modeling, anomaly detection).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Reinforcement Learning</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Learning by trial and error through rewards and punishments in sequential decision-making environments (e.g., game playing, robotics).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>ML Pipeline</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The end-to-end process: Data + Algorithm + Compute → Model → Predictions.

</details>

### Data Leakage

<details>
<summary><b>Data Leakage</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** When information from outside the training set (like test data or future information) leaks into training, causing unrealistically optimistic performance that doesn't generalize to production.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Temporal Leakage</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Using future data to predict the past by shuffling time-series data, which creates patterns that don't exist in real-time deployment.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Test Set Contamination</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** When test set information is used for any decision (like tuning hyperparameters or early stopping), invalidating it as a measure of true generalization.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Preventing Data Leakage</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Fit all preprocessing (scaling, encoding, outlier removal) on training data only, then apply the same transformations to validation and test sets.

</details>

---

### Evaluation Metrics

<details>
<summary><b>Accuracy</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The percentage of correct predictions — `(TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN)` — but can be misleading with imbalanced classes.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Precision</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** How many of the positive predictions were actually correct — `TP / (TP + FP)` — prioritize when false positives are costly (e.g., marking real emails as spam).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Recall (Sensitivity)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** How many of the actual positives were caught — `TP / (TP + FN)` — prioritize when false negatives are costly (e.g., missing cancer in screening).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>F1 Score</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The harmonic mean of precision and recall — `2 × (P × R) / (P + R)` — balances both metrics, ideal for imbalanced classes.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>AUC-ROC</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A threshold-independent metric that measures a model's ability to rank positive examples higher than negative ones; 0.5 = random guessing, 1.0 = perfect ranking.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Measures average prediction error in the same units as the target, penalizing large errors more heavily than MAE.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>MAE (Mean Absolute Error)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Measures average absolute prediction error in the same units as the target; more robust to outliers than RMSE.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>R² (Coefficient of Determination)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The proportion of variance in the target explained by the model — intuitive for stakeholders, ranging from 0 to 1.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Measures error as a percentage of the actual value, making it intuitive for business reporting.

</details>

---

### Underfitting vs Overfitting

<details>
<summary><b>Underfitting (High Bias)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The model is too simple to capture the underlying patterns, resulting in high error on both training and test data.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Overfitting (High Variance)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The model memorizes the training data (including noise) and performs well on train but poorly on test, failing to generalize.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Bias-Variance Tradeoff</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** As model complexity increases, bias decreases but variance increases; the goal is to find the sweet spot that minimizes total error.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Bias-Variance Decomposition</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Total expected error = Bias² + Variance + Irreducible Error (noise inherent in the data that no model can eliminate).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Irreducible Error (σ²)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The noise in the data itself (measurement errors, unmeasured factors) that sets the floor for achievable model performance.

</details>

---

### Cross-Validation

<details>
<summary><b>Train/Validation/Test Split</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The three-way split: Training set fits the model, Validation set tunes hyperparameters, Test set provides the final unbiased performance estimate.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>K-Fold Cross-Validation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Divide data into K folds; train on K-1 folds and validate on the held-out fold, repeating K times to get a reliable performance estimate.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Stratified K-Fold</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Like K-Fold but preserves class proportions in each fold, essential for imbalanced datasets.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** K-Fold where K = N (number of samples); trains on N-1 samples and validates on 1, repeated N times — useful for tiny datasets but computationally expensive.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Nested Cross-Validation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Two-level CV: outer loop evaluates model performance, inner loop tunes hyperparameters — prevents double-dipping and gives unbiased performance estimates.

</details>

---

### Feature Scaling

<details>
<summary><b>Why Feature Scaling Matters</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Features with larger magnitudes dominate distance-based algorithms (like KNN), making scaling essential when features have different units/ranges.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Min-Max Normalization</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Rescales features to a fixed range, usually [0, 1], using `(x - min) / (max - min)`.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Z-Score Standardization</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Transforms features to have zero mean and unit variance using `(x - μ) / σ`; more robust to outliers than Min-Max.

</details>

---

### Curse of Dimensionality

<details>
<summary><b>Curse of Dimensionality</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** In high-dimensional spaces, distances equalize (nearest and farthest become indistinguishable), data becomes sparse, and every point is an outlier.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>KNN Time Complexity</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Naive KNN is O(n·d) per query — with 1M samples and 100 features, that's 100 million operations per query, too slow for production.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>ANN (Approximate Nearest Neighbors)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Sub-linear time algorithms (O(log n)) that sacrifice perfect accuracy for scalability, powering modern vector databases (HNSW, FAISS).

</details>

---

### Distance Metrics

<details>
<summary><b>Euclidean Distance (L2)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The straight-line distance between two points; best for continuous features after scaling.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Manhattan Distance (L1)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The grid-based distance (sum of absolute differences); useful when movement is restricted to axes (robots, sparse data).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Cosine Similarity</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Measures angle (direction) between vectors regardless of magnitude; ideal for text documents and embeddings.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Jaccard Similarity</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Measures overlap between two sets (intersection over union); ideal for binary purchase history or sets.

</details>

---

### Regularization & Model Selection

<details>
<summary><b>Regularization</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Techniques that constrain model complexity to prevent overfitting, slightly increasing bias to significantly reduce variance.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Overfitting Fixes</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** More data, simpler model, regularization, feature selection, early stopping, or cross-validation.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Underfitting Fixes</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** More complex model, more features, reduce regularization, or train longer.

</details>

### Ethics & Pitfalls

<details>
<summary><b>Distribution Shift (Concept Drift)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** When the data distribution changes after deployment (e.g., COVID changed economic patterns), making the model outdated and inaccurate.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Biased Training Data</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Historical data containing societal biases (e.g., discriminatory lending) gets inherited and amplified by the model, creating fairness violations.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Explainability (Interpretability)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The ability to understand why a model made a decision; critical in high-stakes domains like healthcare, finance, and law.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Model Memorization & Privacy</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Models can memorize training data (e.g., medical records), and predictions might inadvertently leak private information.

</details>

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Deep Blue</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>ELIZA</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A 1966 chatbot with scripted pattern-matched responses; not true AI because it had zero understanding and just mirrored user input with simple rules.

### Beyond Supervised/Unsupervised/RL

<details>
<summary><b>Semi-Supervised Learning</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Uses a small amount of labeled data + a large amount of unlabeled data, common in practice because labeling is expensive.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Self-Supervised Learning</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Creates supervision signals from the data itself (e.g., GPT predicts the next word, BERT masks words); no human labels needed.

---

<details>
<summary><b>Transfer Learning</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Pre-training a model on a large dataset (e.g., ResNet on ImageNet) and fine-tuning it on a smaller, task-specific dataset; works great when you have limited data.

</details>
