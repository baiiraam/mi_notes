### Linear Regression

<details>
<summary><b>Linear Model Equation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `ŷ = wᵀx + b` — a weighted sum of input features plus a bias term, where `w` are the slopes/weights and `b` is the intercept.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Matrix Form of Linear Model</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `ŷ = Xw` where `X` is the design matrix (samples × features with a bias column appended) and `w` is the parameter vector.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>MSE Cost Function</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `L(w) = (1/n)‖y − Xw‖²` — the average squared difference between predictions and actual targets.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Normal Equations</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `XᵀXw = Xᵀy` — the system of equations that gives the optimal weights by setting the gradient to zero.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Closed-Form Solution (OLS)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `w* = (XᵀX)⁻¹ Xᵀy` — the analytical solution that finds the weights directly, but fails when `XᵀX` is not invertible (multicollinearity or d > n).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Geometric Interpretation of OLS</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `ŷ = Xw*` is the projection of `y` onto the column space of `X`, and the residual `e = y − ŷ` is perpendicular to every column of `X` (orthogonality).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Probabilistic Interpretation of Linear Regression</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Assuming Gaussian noise `ε ~ N(0, σ²)`, MLE of `w` is equivalent to ordinary least squares, and `σ²* = (1/n)‖y − Xw*‖²`.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Gauss-Markov Theorem</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Under classical OLS assumptions, `w*` is the Best Linear Unbiased Estimator (BLUE) — minimum variance among all linear unbiased estimators.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Polynomial Regression</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Mapping input `x` to feature vector `φ(x) = [1, x, x², ..., xᵈ]` gives non-linear decision boundaries in `x`-space while keeping convex optimization in `w`-space.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Bias-Variance Decomposition</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `MSE = Bias² + Variance + σ²` — as model complexity increases, bias decreases but variance increases, creating the classic tradeoff.

</details>

---

### Regularization

<details>
<summary><b>Regularized Objective</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `argmin_w [Loss(w) + λ·Penalty(w)]` — add a penalty term to the loss to discourage overly complex models and improve generalization.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Ridge Regression (L2)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `argmin_w [Loss(w) + λ·‖w‖₂²]` — shrinks all coefficients smoothly toward zero but rarely sets them exactly to zero; useful for correlated features.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Lasso Regression (L1)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `argmin_w [Loss(w) + λ·‖w‖₁]` — can set coefficients exactly to zero, performing automatic feature selection for sparse models.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>ElasticNet</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `argmin_w [Loss(w) + λ·(ρ·‖w‖₁ + (1−ρ)·‖w‖₂²)]` — combines the sparsity of Lasso with the stability of Ridge; better than pure Lasso for correlated features.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Parameters vs Hyperparameters</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Parameters (like `w`) are learned during training; hyperparameters (like `α`, polynomial degree) are chosen before training using validation.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Grid Search</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Exhaustively evaluates every combination of a fixed set of hyperparameter values; cost grows multiplicatively with the number of hyperparameters.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Random Search</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Samples hyperparameter values from distributions at random; often more efficient than grid search because it explores more distinct values under the same budget.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Nested Cross-Validation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Outer loop evaluates model performance, inner loop tunes hyperparameters; gives an unbiased estimate of the entire model-selection procedure.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Learning Curve</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Model performance as a function of training set size; helps diagnose whether more data would help (validation curve still rising) or if the model is noise-limited.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>High Bias (Underfitting) Diagnosis</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Training and validation scores are both low and close together; fix by increasing model flexibility, adding features, or reducing regularization.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>High Variance (Overfitting) Diagnosis</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Training score is high but validation score is much lower (large gap); fix with more data, stronger regularization, simpler model, or early stopping.

</details>

---

### Logistic Regression

<details>
<summary><b>Logistic Regression Model</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `p(y=1|x) = σ(wᵀx + b) = 1/(1 + e⁻(wᵀx+b))` — maps a linear score to a probability between 0 and 1 using the sigmoid function.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Binary Cross-Entropy Loss</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `−[y·log(p) + (1−y)·log(1−p)]` — penalizes confident wrong predictions heavily; derived from MLE under a Bernoulli observation model.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Regularized Logistic Regression</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** L2 shrinks coefficients smoothly; L1 drives coefficients to zero (sparse selection); ElasticNet is a mix; `C = 1/λ` in scikit-learn.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Logit (Log-Odds) Interpretation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `logit(p) = log(p/(1−p)) = wᵀx + b` — coefficients are linear effects on log-odds, not directly on probability.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Multiclass Logistic Regression</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** One-vs-Rest trains K binary classifiers; Softmax/Multinomial uses `softmax(zⱼ) = exp(zⱼ)/Σ exp(zⱼ)` for natural multiclass probabilities.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Threshold and Decision Making</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Default threshold is 0.5; increasing the threshold gives higher precision and lower recall; choose threshold on validation data based on real costs.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>ROC Curve & AUC</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** ROC plots TPR vs FPR across all thresholds; AUC is threshold-independent, with 0.5 = random classifier and 1.0 = perfect classifier.

</details>

---

### Naive Bayes

<details>
<summary><b>Discriminative vs Generative Models</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Discriminative (like Logistic Regression) models `P(y|x)` directly; generative (like Naive Bayes) models `P(x|y)` and `P(y)` and uses Bayes' rule.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Bayes' Rule for Classification</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `P(y|x) = P(x|y)·P(y) / P(x)` — choose the class that maximizes `P(x|y)·P(y)` since `P(x)` is the same for all classes.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>The "Naive" Assumption</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Features are conditionally independent given the class: `P(x₁, x₂, ..., x_d|y) = Πⱼ P(xⱼ|y)` — often false in real data but still works well in practice.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Naive Bayes Variants</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Gaussian NB (continuous features), Bernoulli NB (binary indicators), Multinomial NB (counts/frequencies — the standard first choice for text).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Multinomial Naive Bayes</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Represents documents as word count vectors and scores as `score(y) = log P(y) + Σⱼ countⱼ·log P(wordⱼ|y)` using log probabilities to avoid underflow.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Laplace Smoothing</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `P(word|class) = (count + α) / (total + α·|V|)` — prevents unseen words from making a whole class impossible by adding pseudo-counts to every vocabulary word.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Logistic Regression vs Naive Bayes</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Logistic regression is discriminative with a linear decision boundary; Naive Bayes is generative with a conditional independence assumption; try both as baselines on text.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Bag-of-Words (BoW)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Ignores word order, counts how often each vocabulary term appears in a document — simple, interpretable, fast, and often strong.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>TF-IDF</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `tf-idf(term, doc) = tf × log(N / df)` — counts words but discounts very common corpus words like "the"; often improves linear text classifiers.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Text Classification Pipeline</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `raw documents → tokenize → vectorize → classifier → label` — always fit the vectorizer on training data only to prevent leakage.

</details>

### Diagnosis Quick Reference

<details>
<summary><b>Underfitting vs Overfitting Diagnosis</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Symptom | Diagnosis | Fix |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| Train low, Val low, small gap | **High Bias** | More complexity, more features, reduce regularization |
| Train high, Val lower, large gap | **High Variance** | More data, stronger regularization, simpler model |
| Valid still rising with data | **Data-limited** | Collect more data |
| Both plateau below target | **Noise-limited** | Improve labels/features |

</details>
