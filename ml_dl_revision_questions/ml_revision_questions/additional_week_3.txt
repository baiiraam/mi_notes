### Data Preparation

<details>
<summary><b>Data Preparation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The process of cleaning, transforming, and organizing raw data into a format suitable for analysis and modeling — consuming 60–80% of a data scientist's time.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Data Quality Issues</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Missing values, duplicates, outliers, inconsistent formatting, wrong data types, and unencoded categories that silently break groupBy, joins, and model features.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Data Preparation Pipeline</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** An iterative process: Raw Data → Inspect → Clean → Impute → Encode → Visualize — looping back as new issues are discovered.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Garbage In, Garbage Out</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The quality of your output is strictly limited by the quality of your input — bad data leads to bad models regardless of algorithm choice.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Types of Duplicates</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Exact duplicates (identical rows), key-field duplicates (same ID, different attributes), and near-duplicates (fuzzy matches like 'Jon Smith' vs 'John Smith').

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Outliers</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Data points that differ significantly from other observations, detected via Z-score (normal data), IQR (skewed data), or Isolation Forest.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Outlier Handling Strategies</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Remove (if error confirmed), impute (replace with median), winsorize (cap at thresholds), separate (compare with/without), transform (log/Box-Cox), or keep (fraud/rare disease).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Missing Value Mechanisms (MCAR, MAR, MNAR)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** MCAR (missing completely at random — safe to delete), MAR (missing depends on observed variables — impute valid), MNAR (missing depends on the missing value itself — highest bias risk).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Simple Imputation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Mean (symmetric data), Median (skewed data), Mode (categorical), or Constant ('Unknown') — fast but distorts distributions and reduces variance.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Advanced Imputation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** KNN imputation (similar rows), MICE (iterative modeling), and forward/backward fill (time series) — more accurate but computationally expensive.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Missingness Indicator</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A binary column recording whether a value was missing — useful when the FACT of being missing predicts your target (MNAR).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Label Encoding</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Assigns integers to categories (e.g., 'USA'→0, 'Canada'→1) — implies ordering, use only for tree-based models or truly ordinal data.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>One-Hot Encoding</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Creates a binary column for each unique category — standard for nominal variables; use `drop_first=True` to avoid multicollinearity.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Target Encoding</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Replaces each category with the mean of the target variable for that category — powerful but requires K-fold cross-fitting to prevent leakage.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Frequency Encoding</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Replaces each category with its count or proportion in the dataset — no target leakage, but loses distinction between categories with same frequency.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Dummy Variable Trap</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** When K categories create K dummy columns that are perfectly collinear (sum to 1); fix by dropping one column (`drop_first=True`).

</details>

---

### Feature Engineering

<details>
<summary><b>Feature Engineering</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Using domain knowledge to create, transform, or select input variables that make machine learning algorithms work better — Andrew Ng: "Applied ML is basically feature engineering."

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Min-Max Normalization</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `x' = (x − min)/(max − min)` — scales to [0,1] range; best for neural networks/KNN/SVM but sensitive to outliers.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Standardization (Z-score)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `x' = (x − μ)/σ` — transforms to mean=0, std=1; the most commonly used scaling method; robust to outliers.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Robust Scaling</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `x' = (x − median)/IQR` — uses median and IQR; resistant to outliers; best for data with outliers you cannot remove.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Log Transform</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Reduces right-skew (income, prices, counts), stabilizes variance, and improves linear model fit; use `log(x+1)` when data includes zeros.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Box-Cox Transform</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `((x^λ − 1)/λ)` — finds optimal λ automatically; data must be positive; use `PowerTransformer(method='box-cox')`.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Yeo-Johnson Transform</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Modified Box-Cox that handles zero and negative values too; use `PowerTransformer(method='yeo-johnson')`.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Binning (Discretization)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Equal-width (risk: sparse bins if skewed), equal-frequency (quantile bins, better for skewed), or domain-knowledge (most interpretable, business rules).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Polynomial Features</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Capture non-linear relationships by adding powers (`x²` for U-shapes) and cross-products (`x₁×x₂` for interactions).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Ratio Features</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Debt-to-Income, Credit Utilization, Conversion Rate — capture relative relationships that raw absolute values alone don't reveal.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Temporal Features</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Extract year, month, day_of_week, hour, is_weekend; cyclically encode hours/months with sin/cos to preserve circular structure.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Lag Features</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Look backward in time (sales_lag1 = yesterday, sales_lag7 = same day last week) to capture autocorrelation and seasonality.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Rolling Window Statistics</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Rolling aggregations over a window (7-day average, 30-day max) to capture trends, volatility, and momentum.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Text Feature Engineering</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Bag-of-Words (simple counts), TF-IDF (downweights common words), or Embeddings (dense vectors of meaning) — TF-IDF is standard baseline for document classification.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Aggregation Features</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Group-level statistics per entity (total_spend, avg_spend, num_purchases) computed on training rows only to prevent leakage.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Feature Selection Methods</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Filter (statistical scoring — fast, model-agnostic), Wrapper (RFE — accurate but slow), or Embedded (Lasso/tree importance — fast and accurate but model-specific).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Feature Store</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** For large-scale production: pre-compute and cache features to avoid recomputing at every inference request.

</details>

### Data Visualization

<details>
<summary><b>Anscombe's Quartet</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Four datasets with IDENTICAL summary statistics (mean, variance, correlation, regression line) but completely different when plotted — proving visualization catches what numbers hide.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Data-Ink Ratio (Tufte)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** `Data-Ink Ratio = Ink used to display data / Total ink used` — "Above all else show the data" — remove chartjunk, maximize data ink.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Gestalt Principles for Visualization</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Proximity (close = related), Continuity (eyes follow lines), Similarity (same color = same category), Closure (brain completes shapes), Enclosure (box = group), Figure/Ground (main vs background).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Sequential Colormap</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Ordered, numeric data (Blues) — use for low to high counts, income, density.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Diverging Colormap</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Meaningful midpoint (RdYlBu) — use for correlation (-1 to 1), temperature anomaly, sentiment.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Qualitative Colormap</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Nominal categories (tab10) — use for department, country, species (no order).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Colorblind Considerations</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** 8% of men and 0.5% of women are colorblind — avoid red+green, use colorblind-safe palettes like Viridis, Cividis, or ColorBrewer.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Chart Selection — Bar Chart</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Best for comparing values across categories; bars must start at zero and be sorted by value.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Chart Selection — Line Chart</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Best for trends over time; exploits Gestalt continuity — the eye connects points into trends naturally.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Chart Selection — Scatter Plot</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Best for relationship between two numeric variables; reveals correlation, clusters, and non-linear patterns.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Chart Selection — Box Plot</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Best for spread, median, and outliers per group — shows quartiles, range, and outliers in one chart.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Visualization Anti-Patterns</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Truncated Y-axis (exaggerates differences), 3D charts (distorts perception), chartjunk (visual noise), too many colors (>7 impossible to distinguish), wrong chart type, missing context (no labels/units/source).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Storytelling with Data</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Context (who+why) → Conflict (the question) → Resolution (answer obvious in 5 seconds) → Action (what the audience should do next).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Dashboard Design</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Top-left = most important KPI; flow left-to-right, top-to-bottom; group related charts with whitespace; show 1–2 key numbers per section.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Geospatial Visualization Types</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Choropleth (color-fill regions, one value per region), Bubble (points sized by value, city-level data), Heatmap/Density (thousands of points, reveals hot spots).

</details>

### Data Quality Issues

<details>
<summary><b>5 Common Data Quality Issues</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

1. **Missing Values** (NaN, NULL, sentinel values like -999)
2. **Duplicates** (exact, key-field, near-duplicates)
3. **Outliers** (extreme values from error or rare events)
4. **Inconsistent Format** ('USA', 'U.S.A', 'us')
5. **Wrong Dtype** (zip codes as floats, dates as strings)

</details>

---

### Missing Value Mechanisms

<details>
<summary><b>MCAR, MAR, MNAR</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Type | Meaning | Example | Action |
|------|---------|---------|--------|
| **MCAR** | Missing completely at random | Sensor randomly drops 5% of readings | Safe to delete |
| **MAR** | Missing depends on observed variables | Men less likely to report weight | Impute validly |
| **MNAR** | Missing depends on missing value itself | High earners skip salary field | Highest bias risk |

</details>

---

### Missing Value Decision Thresholds

<details>
<summary><b>Thresholds</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Missing Rate | Recommended Strategy |
|--------------|---------------------|
| **< 5%** | Almost any strategy works |
| **5–20%** | Impute carefully |
| **20–40%** | Consider adding missingness indicator |
| **> 40%** | Consider dropping column |

</details>

---

### Scaling Methods

<details>
<summary><b>Scaling Methods</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Method | Formula | Best For | Outlier Sensitivity |
|--------|---------|----------|---------------------|
| **Min-Max** | `(x − min)/(max − min)` | NNs, KNN, SVM, images | High |
| **Standardization** | `(x − μ)/σ` | Linear/logistic regression, PCA, SVM | Low (most common) |
| **Robust Scaling** | `(x − median)/IQR` | Data with outliers | Very low |

</details>

---

### Transformations

<details>
<summary><b>Log & Power Transforms</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Transform | Formula | Use When |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| **Log** | `log(x)` | Right-skewed data (income, prices) |
| **Log1p** | `log(x+1)` | Data includes zeros |
| **Box-Cox** | `((x^λ − 1)/λ)` | Positive data only, finds optimal λ |
| **Yeo-Johnson** | Modified Box-Cox | Handles zero and negative values |

</details>

---

### Encoding Methods

<details>
<summary><b>Encoding Methods</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Method | When to Use | Risk |
|--------|-------------|------|
| **Label Encoding** | Tree-based models, ordinal data | Implies order |
| **One-Hot Encoding** | Nominal, few categories (<15) | Column explosion |
| **Target Encoding** | High-cardinality nominal | Leakage risk (use K-fold) |
| **Frequency Encoding** | High-cardinality nominal | Loses category distinction |

</details>

---

### Encoding Decision Guide

<details>
<summary><b>Choosing an Encoding Strategy</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Scenario | Recommended Encoding |
|----------|---------------------|
| Binary feature (yes/no) | Binary (0/1) |
| Nominal, few categories (<15) | One-Hot Encoding |
| Ordinal with known order | Ordinal Encoding |
| High-cardinality nominal | Target or Frequency Encoding |
| Tree-based model | Label / Ordinal Encoding |
| Linear model / SVM / Neural Net | One-Hot Encoding |

</details>

---

### Feature Scaling Summary

<details>
<summary><b>Scaling Summary</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Method | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|
| **Min-Max** | Neural networks, KNN, SVM, image data |
| **Standardization** | Linear/logistic regression, PCA, SVM (most common) |
| **Robust Scaling** | Data with outliers you cannot remove |

</details>

### Feature Selection Methods

<details>
<summary><b>Filter, Wrapper, Embedded</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Method | How It Works | Pros | Cons |
|--------|--------------|------|------|
| **Filter** | Statistical scores (correlation, chi²) | Fast, model-agnostic | Ignores interactions |
| **Wrapper** | Try subsets, measure performance (RFE) | Accurate, considers interactions | Slow, overfitting risk |
| **Embedded** | Built into training (Lasso, tree importance) | Fast and accurate | Model-specific |

</details>

### Bar Chart Best Practices

<details>
