### L1 — CNN Fundamentals

<details>
<summary><b>Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A neural network architecture that uses local receptive fields and shared weights to exploit spatial structure, drastically reducing parameters and enabling translation invariance.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Local Receptive Field</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Each neuron is connected only to a local patch of the input, capturing nearby spatial patterns (edges, textures) rather than the whole image.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Weight Sharing</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The same filter (kernel) is applied across the entire input, dramatically reducing the number of parameters and enabling translation invariance.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Translation Invariance</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A feature can be detected regardless of its position in the image because the same filter slides across all spatial locations.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>1D Convolution</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A kernel slides across 1D data (text, audio, time-series) to extract local patterns like rising edges or word combinations, regardless of their position in the sequence.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>2D Convolution</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The fundamental image operation where a kernel slides over height and width, computing dot products to produce feature maps that detect spatial patterns.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Padding</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Adding zeros (or other values) around the input to preserve spatial dimensions and prevent edge information from being lost during convolution.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Valid Padding (No Padding)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** No zeros added; output shrinks by `(K-1)` dimensions, and border pixels are under-represented.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Same Padding (Zero Padding)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Pads with zeros so output size equals input size (when stride=1), preserving spatial dimensions for deep architectures.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Stride</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The step size of the kernel as it slides; stride > 1 downsamples the feature map, reducing resolution and computation.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Max Pooling</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Selects the maximum value in each pooling window, retaining the strongest feature response and providing spatial invariance.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Average Pooling</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Computes the average value in each pooling window, smoothing the feature map and aggregating global context.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Receptive Field</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The region of the input that a CNN feature "sees"; shallow layers see local details, deep layers see global context through accumulated convolutions.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Dilation (Atrous Convolution)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Inserts holes between kernel weights to expand the receptive field without adding parameters or reducing resolution, essential for segmentation.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Transposed Convolution (Deconvolution)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A learnable upsampling operation that projects low-resolution features to higher resolution by spreading activations with a learned kernel.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Bilinear Interpolation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A non-learnable upsampling method that computes smooth pixel values using weighted averages of the 2×2 neighborhood, avoiding checkerboard artifacts.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Nearest Neighbor Upsampling</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The simplest upsampling method that repeats the nearest pixel value; fast but produces blocky artifacts.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Checkerboard Artifacts</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Uneven overlapping patterns in transposed convolution outputs, mitigated by using smaller strides or pairing with bilinear interpolation.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Batch Normalization in CNNs</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Normalizes layer activations across the batch to stabilize training, enabling higher learning rates and acting as a mild regularizer.

</details>

---

### L2 — Computer Vision Tasks

<details>
<summary><b>Object Detection</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Locating objects with bounding boxes and assigning class labels, answering "what" and "where" in an image.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Semantic Segmentation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Labeling every pixel with a category (e.g., road, car, person), but not distinguishing between individual objects of the same class.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Instance Segmentation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Labeling every pixel and distinguishing between individual objects of the same class (e.g., each separate pedestrian), combining detection and segmentation.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>IoU (Intersection over Union)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The ratio of overlap to union between predicted and ground truth boxes; IoU ≥ 0.5 typically counts as a True Positive detection.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Precision (Detection)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Of all detections made, how many are correct — `TP / (TP + FP)`; high precision means few false positives.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Recall (Detection)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Of all ground truth objects, how many were detected — `TP / (TP + FN)`; high recall means few false negatives.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>mAP (Mean Average Precision)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The primary object detection metric; the average of AP values across all classes, balancing precision and recall across confidence thresholds.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>OKS (Object Keypoint Similarity)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The keypoint detection metric analogous to IoU, measuring the distance between predicted and ground truth keypoints with object-scale normalization.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Faster R-CNN</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A two-stage detector that first proposes regions of interest (RPN) and then classifies + refines each proposal, prioritizing high accuracy.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Region Proposal Network (RPN)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A convolutional layer in Faster R-CNN that scans the feature map to generate candidate object proposals using anchor boxes.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Anchor Boxes</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Multi-scale, multi-aspect-ratio reference boxes used by RPN to handle variation in object size and shape.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>ROI Pooling</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Hard quantization of ROI coordinates to a fixed-size grid, causing misalignment that hurts pixel-level precision.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>ROI Align</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Uses bilinear interpolation instead of rounding to preserve precise spatial information, essential for Mask R-CNN's pixel-perfect masks.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>YOLO (You Only Look Once)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A single-stage detector that frames detection as a regression problem, predicting boxes and classes directly from a grid, prioritizing real-time speed.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>U-Net</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A symmetric encoder-decoder architecture for segmentation with skip connections that preserve high-resolution spatial details lost during downsampling.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Skip Connections (U-Net)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Direct links between encoder and decoder layers that recover spatial resolution, preventing the "vanishing" of structural information in deep networks.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Mask R-CNN</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Extends Faster R-CNN with a third branch for pixel-level masks, performing classification, bounding box regression, and mask prediction simultaneously.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Multi-Task Learning</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Learning multiple related tasks (classification, detection, segmentation) with a shared backbone, forcing the network to learn robust unified representations.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Weighted Cross-Entropy</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A segmentation loss that assigns higher weights to minority classes (e.g., tumors) to prevent the model from ignoring rare objects.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Dice Loss</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A segmentation loss based on the Dice coefficient (`2|A∩B|/(|A|+|B|)`) that is robust to class imbalance and widely used in medical imaging.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Focal Loss</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A loss that down-weights easy, well-classified background examples, forcing the model to focus on "hard" pixels in highly imbalanced datasets.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Feature Pyramid Network (FPN)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A multi-scale feature hierarchy that builds feature maps at different resolutions to detect objects consistently regardless of their size.

</details>

---

### L3 — Keypoint Detection & Pose Estimation

<details>
<summary><b>Keypoint Detection</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Detecting distinctive structural points (joints, facial landmarks) to represent complex geometry as a sparse, compact skeleton.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Heatmap-Based Keypoint Detection</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Represents each keypoint as a 2D Gaussian distribution (heatmap), using MSE loss to compare predicted vs ground truth distributions for robust localization.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Regression-Based Keypoint Detection</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Directly predicts `(x, y)` coordinates; faster but more sensitive to outliers than heatmap-based approaches.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>OpenPose</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A bottom-up pose estimation method that detects all joints first, then uses Part Affinity Fields (PAFs) to associate them into individual skeletons.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Part Affinity Fields (PAFs)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Vector fields learned by OpenPose that encode limb orientation, solving the association problem by grouping correct joints to the correct person in multi-person scenes.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Bottom-Up Pose Estimation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Detects all body parts first, then associates them into individuals; scales well with crowd density (OpenPose).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Top-Down Pose Estimation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Detects a person first (person detector), then estimates keypoints within the cropped region; higher precision but slower for multiple people (HRNet).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>HRNet</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Maintains high-resolution representations throughout the network using parallel multi-resolution streams, achieving state-of-the-art keypoint precision.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Action Recognition</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Analyzing keypoint movement across frames to classify actions (walking, waving), using RNNs/LSTMs or Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to model temporal dynamics.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) for Pose</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Treats the skeleton as a graph (joints = nodes, limbs = edges) and learns spatial-temporal features by traversing the human graph structure over time.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>3D Pose Estimation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Mapping 2D keypoints to 3D coordinates using 2D-to-3D lifting networks or direct regression, often constrained by anatomical priors (bone length consistency).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>2D-to-3D Lifting</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A two-stage approach: first detect 2D keypoints (HRNet), then use a separate network to "lift" them into 3D space.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Pose-Guided Image Synthesis</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Using keypoints as a structural scaffold to generate new images (e.g., pose transfer, virtual try-on) via GANs or diffusion models conditioned on skeleton data.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Model Quantization</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Converting high-precision weights (FP32) to lower precision (INT8) to accelerate inference on edge devices for real-time pose estimation.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Backbone Pruning</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Removing redundant filters from the network to reduce compute while maintaining keypoint precision for mobile deployment.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>COCO Dataset</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The gold standard for multi-person pose estimation, featuring challenging crowded scenes and varied object scales.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>MPII Human Pose Dataset</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A high-quality dataset focused on diverse, complex, and real-world human poses for keypoint detection.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Human3.6M Dataset</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A large-scale dataset specifically for 3D human pose estimation, captured in a controlled laboratory environment with multiple camera views.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Ethical Considerations in Pose Tracking</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Pose estimation enables gait recognition (unique identification), raising privacy concerns, and datasets often exhibit demographic bias, requiring fairness by design.

</details>

### CNN Output Size Formula

<details>
<summary><b>Convolution Output Size</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Formula** | `O = (I - K + 2P) / S + 1` |
| **Where** | I = input size, K = kernel size, P = padding, S = stride |
| **Example** | 32×32 input, 3×3 kernel, padding=1, stride=1 → 32×32 |

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Pooling Output Size</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Formula** | `O = (I - K) / S + 1` |
| **Note** | Pooling has no learnable parameters |

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Transposed Convolution Output Size</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Formula** | `O = (I - 1)S + K - 2P` |

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Effective Receptive Field with Dilation</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Formula** | `K_eff = K + (K-1)(d-1)` |
| **Example** | 3×3 kernel, dilation=2 → effective 5×5 (still 9 parameters) |

</details>

---

### IoU Calculation

<details>
<summary><b>IoU Formula</b> — Try to recall first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Formula** | `IoU = Area(Prediction ∩ GroundTruth) / Area(Prediction ∪ GroundTruth)` |
| **True Positive Threshold** | Usually IoU ≥ 0.5 |
| **IoU = 1** | Perfect overlap |
| **IoU = 0** | No overlap |

</details>

### Detection Metrics

<details>
<summary><b>Precision, Recall, F1</b> — Try to recall formulas first</summary>

| Metric | Formula | What It Measures |
|--------|---------|------------------|
| **Precision** | `TP / (TP + FP)` | How many detections are correct |
| **Recall** | `TP / (TP + FN)` | How many objects were found |
| **F1 Score** | `2·P·R / (P + R)` | Harmonic mean; balanced measure |

</details>

</details>
