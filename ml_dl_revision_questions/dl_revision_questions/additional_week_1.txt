### L2 — MLP Fundamentals & MLE

<details>
<summary><b>MLE (Maximum Likelihood Estimation)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A statistical framework where we find parameters that maximize the probability of observing our training data.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Negative Log-Likelihood</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** We minimize the negative log of the likelihood because it turns products into sums and maximization into minimization (easier for optimization).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>MSE (Mean Squared Error)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Under MLE, assuming Gaussian noise in regression, minimizing MSE is equivalent to maximizing likelihood.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Under MLE, assuming a Bernoulli distribution for binary classification, minimizing BCE is equivalent to maximizing likelihood.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Categorical Cross-Entropy (CCE)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Under MLE, assuming a Multinomial distribution for multiclass classification, minimizing CCE is equivalent to maximizing likelihood.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Forward Pass</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The process of passing input data through the network layer by layer to generate a prediction.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Activation Function</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The non-linear function (like ReLU or Sigmoid) that gives neural networks the power to approximate complex functions.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Xavier Initialization</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Weight initialization designed for Sigmoid/Tanh activations to maintain variance across layers.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>He Initialization</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Weight initialization designed for ReLU activations; scales weights by a factor of 2 to compensate for ReLU zeroing out half the inputs.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Loss Landscape</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The high-dimensional surface of the loss function; for deep networks it's non-convex with saddle points and plateaus.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Saddle Point</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A point where the gradient is zero but it's neither a local minimum nor maximum — more common than local minima in high dimensions.

</details>

---

### L3 — Activation Functions

<details>
<summary><b>ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The default hidden-layer activation — outputs 0 for negatives, x for positives; cheap, sparse, and avoids vanishing gradients.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Dying ReLU</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** When a neuron's input is consistently negative, it outputs 0 and its gradient is 0, making it permanently "dead."

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Leaky ReLU</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Fixes Dying ReLU by allowing a small, non-zero gradient for negative inputs (e.g., `f(z) = max(0.01z, z)`).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>ELU (Exponential Linear Unit)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A smoother variant of ReLU with a negative region that pushes mean activations closer to zero.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>GELU (Gaussian Error Linear Unit)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The standard for Transformers — a smooth, probabilistic activation that weights inputs by their magnitude.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Sigmoid</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Squashes outputs to (0,1); historically popular but suffers from vanishing gradients and isn't zero-centered.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Tanh</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Squashes outputs to (-1,1); zero-centered but still suffers from vanishing gradients.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Vanishing Gradients</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Gradients become exponentially small through many layers, preventing early layers from learning; fixed by ReLU, Batch Norm, and residual connections.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Sparsity</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** In ReLU, many neurons output 0, creating sparse representations that are computationally efficient and act as implicit regularization.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Softmax</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Turns logits into a valid probability distribution (all values between 0 and 1, summing to 1) for multiclass classification.

</details>

### Key Formulas

<details>
<summary><b>MSE (Mean Squared Error)</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

**Formula:** `MSE = (1/N) Σ (ŷ - y)²`

**Description:** Regression loss — MLE under Gaussian assumption.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>BCE (Binary Cross-Entropy)</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

**Formula:** `BCE = -[y·log(ŷ) + (1-y)·log(1-ŷ)]`

**Description:** Binary classification loss — MLE under Bernoulli assumption.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>CCE (Categorical Cross-Entropy)</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

**Formula:** `CCE = -log(ŷ_target)`

**Description:** Multiclass classification loss — MLE under Multinomial assumption.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Softmax</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

**Formula:** `Softmax(z)_i = e^z_i / Σ e^z_j`

**Description:** Turns logits into a valid probability distribution.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>ReLU</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

**Formula:** `ReLU(z) = max(0, z)`

**Description:** Default hidden activation; cheap, sparse, avoids vanishing gradients.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Sigmoid</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

**Formula:** `Sigmoid(z) = 1/(1 + e^(-z))`

**Description:** Binary output activation; squashes to (0,1).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Tanh</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

**Formula:** `Tanh(z) = (e^z - e^(-z))/(e^z + e^(-z))`

**Description:** Zero-centered alternative to Sigmoid; squashes to (-1,1).

</details>
