### L1 — Backpropagation

<details>
<summary><b>Backpropagation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The core algorithm that efficiently computes gradients of the loss with respect to every weight using the chain rule, enabling neural network training.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Chain Rule</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The mathematical foundation that decomposes complex nested functions into a sequence of local derivatives, allowing gradients to flow backward through the network.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Computational Graph</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A directed acyclic graph (DAG) where nodes represent operations and edges represent tensor flow, enabling automatic differentiation by traversing the graph in reverse.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Local Gradient</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The derivative of a primitive operation's output with respect to its input, pre-defined for each node type (addition, multiplication, max, etc.) to enable modular backpropagation.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Gradient Aggregation (Branching)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** When a variable feeds into multiple downstream operations, the total gradient is the sum of gradients from each branch (multivariate chain rule).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Jacobian Matrix</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A matrix of all first-order partial derivatives for vector-valued functions, forming the foundation for gradient propagation through layers.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Automatic Differentiation (AD)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The automated process of computing gradients by recording operations in a computational graph and applying the chain rule in reverse (backpropagation).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Forward Mode AD</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Propagates derivatives forward alongside the forward pass; efficient when the number of inputs is small.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Reverse Mode AD</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Propagates derivatives backward after a forward pass; extremely efficient for scalar outputs (loss) with many inputs (weights) — this is backpropagation.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>MSE Gradient</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The gradient is proportional to the prediction error `(ŷ - y)`, meaning large errors produce large updates and the update slows as the model approaches the target.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>BCE with Sigmoid Gradient</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The gradient is `(ŷ - y)` — identical to MSE with linear output — but BCE avoids saturation in classification tasks, leading to faster learning.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Gradient Checking</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A debugging technique that numerically approximates gradients using finite differences to verify the correctness of analytical backpropagation implementation.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Activation Checkpointing</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A memory-saving technique that recomputes intermediate activations during the backward pass instead of storing them all, trading compute for memory.

</details>

---

### L2 — Optimization

<details>
<summary><b>Optimization</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The process of finding weights that minimize the loss function by navigating a high-dimensional, non-convex landscape using gradient-based methods.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>SGD (Stochastic Gradient Descent)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The simplest optimizer that updates weights using the negative gradient of the loss on a mini-batch.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Momentum</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Accumulates a moving average of past gradients to dampen oscillations in steep directions and accelerate progress in consistent directions.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>RMSProp</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Adaptive learning rate optimizer that normalizes gradients by a moving average of their squared magnitudes, making large updates for infrequent gradients and small updates for frequent ones.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Adam</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The default optimizer that combines Momentum (moving average of gradients) and RMSProp (moving average of squared gradients) with bias correction for robust per-parameter scaling.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Learning Rate Schedule</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A dynamic strategy that varies the learning rate during training, starting high to explore and decreasing to fine-tune near the minimum.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Cosine Annealing</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A smooth learning rate schedule that reduces the rate from maximum to minimum following a cosine curve.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>ReduceLROnPlateau</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** An adaptive schedule that monitors validation loss and reduces the learning rate when the metric stops improving.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Warm-up</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Gradually increasing the learning rate from a very small value to the target rate over the first iterations, preventing early instability in adaptive optimizers.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Batch Size Effects</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Small batches add stochastic noise that helps escape sharp minima (better generalization), while large batches provide accurate gradients but converge to sharp minima (worse generalization).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Linear Scaling Rule</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** When increasing batch size by `k`, multiply the learning rate by `k` to maintain consistent gradient variance.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Hessian Matrix</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A matrix of second-order partial derivatives capturing the curvature of the loss surface; its size (`N²`) makes it infeasible for deep learning.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Newton's Method</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A second-order optimization method that uses the Hessian to move directly toward the minimum of the local quadratic approximation (`w ← w - H⁻¹·g`), eliminating the need for a learning rate.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Quasi-Newton Methods (L-BFGS)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Approximate the Hessian inverse without computing the full matrix, offering a memory-efficient alternative for smaller models.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Condition Number</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The ratio of the largest to smallest Hessian eigenvalue; high values indicate "narrow valleys" where gradient descent struggles.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Preconditioning</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Adaptive optimizers like Adam perform implicit preconditioning by scaling updates, effectively "stretching" flat directions and "compressing" steep ones.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Batch Normalization (BN)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Normalizes layer inputs across the batch dimension to zero mean and unit variance, smoothing the loss landscape and enabling higher learning rates.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Internal Covariate Shift</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** The changing distribution of layer inputs as previous layers update during training, forcing the optimizer to constantly adapt to a "moving target."

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Gradient Clipping</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A stability technique that rescales gradients when their norm exceeds a threshold (norm clipping) or caps individual values, preventing exploding gradients.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Data Parallelism</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Splitting a batch across multiple GPUs and averaging gradients before updating weights (All-Reduce).

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Model Parallelism</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Splitting the network layers across multiple devices, requiring synchronization during both forward and backward passes.

</details>

---

### L3 — Regularization

<details>
<summary><b>Regularization</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Techniques that constrain the model's capacity to prevent overfitting, slightly increasing bias to significantly reduce variance.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Dropout</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Randomly setting a fraction of neurons to zero during training to prevent co-adaptation, approximating an ensemble of subnetworks.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>L2 Regularization (Weight Decay)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Adds a penalty proportional to the squared magnitude of weights to the loss, encouraging smaller, smoother weights that generalize better.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>L1 Regularization (Lasso)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Adds a penalty proportional to the absolute sum of weights, pushing weights exactly to zero for automatic feature selection.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Early Stopping</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Monitors validation loss and halts training when it stops improving, capturing the "sweet spot" before overfitting begins.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Data Augmentation</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Artificially expands the training dataset with label-preserving transformations to increase diversity and improve invariance.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>MixUp</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Creates synthetic training examples by linearly combining pairs of inputs and their labels, smoothing decision boundaries and reducing overconfidence.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Layer Normalization (LN)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Normalizes across the feature dimension per sample instead of the batch, making it ideal for sequences and Transformers.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Sharp Minimum</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A loss minimum where small parameter changes cause large loss increases, indicating overfitting to training noise.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Flat Minimum</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A loss minimum where loss remains low across a wide parameter range, indicating a robust, generalizable model.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Bagging (Bootstrap Aggregating)</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Trains multiple models on different data subsets and averages their predictions, reducing variance without increasing bias.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Boosting</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** Sequentially trains models where each focuses on correcting previous errors, reducing both bias and variance but more prone to overfitting.

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>AdamW</b> — Try your own 1-sentence summary first</summary>

**Model Answer:** A version of Adam that decouples weight decay from the gradient update, preventing the decay from being disproportionately dampened by adaptive scaling.

</details>

### Backpropagation Formulas

<details>
<summary><b>MSE Gradient</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Loss** | `L = (1/N) Σ (ŷ - y)²` |
| **Gradient** | `∂L/∂w = (ŷ - y) · x` |
| **Key Insight** | Large errors = large updates; slows near target |

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>BCE with Sigmoid Gradient</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Loss** | `L = -[y·log(ŷ) + (1-y)·log(1-ŷ)]` |
| **Gradient** | `∂L/∂z = (ŷ - y)` (identical to MSE linear) |
| **Key Insight** | Avoids saturation; faster learning in classification |

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Chain Rule for Weight Gradient</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

**Formula:** `∂L/∂w_ij^(l) = δ_i^(l) · a_j^(l-1)`

**Where:** `δ_i^(l)` is the local error of neuron `i` in layer `l`

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Backpropagation Error Propagation</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

**Formula:** `δ^(l) = ((W^(l+1))ᵀ · δ^(l+1)) ⊙ f'(z^(l))`

**Explanation:** Error propagates backward through the weight matrix and is element-wise multiplied by the activation derivative.

</details>

---

### Optimizer Update Rules

<details>
<summary><b>SGD</b> — Try to recall the update rule first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Update** | `w ← w - η · ∂L/∂w` |
| **Key Hyperparameter** | Learning rate `η` |

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>SGD with Momentum</b> — Try to recall the update rule first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Velocity Update** | `v ← γv + η · ∂L/∂w` |
| **Weight Update** | `w ← w - v` |
| **Key Hyperparameter** | Momentum `γ` (default: 0.9) |

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>RMSProp</b> — Try to recall the update rule first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Cache Update** | `E[g²] ← βE[g²] + (1-β)g²` |
| **Weight Update** | `w ← w - η·g / √(E[g²] + ε)` |
| **Key Hyperparameters** | `β ≈ 0.9`, `ε ≈ 1e-8` |

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Adam</b> — Try to recall the update rule first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **First Moment** | `m ← β₁m + (1-β₁)g` |
| **Second Moment** | `v ← β₂v + (1-β₂)g²` |
| **Bias Correction** | `m̂ ← m/(1-β₁ᵗ)`, `v̂ ← v/(1-β₂ᵗ)` |
| **Weight Update** | `w ← w - η·m̂/(√v̂ + ε)` |
| **Key Hyperparameters** | `β₁=0.9`, `β₂=0.999`, `ε=1e-8` |

</details>

---

### Regularization Techniques

<details>
<summary><b>L2 Regularization (Weight Decay)</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Penalty** | `λ · Σ w²` |
| **Gradient Update** | `w ← w - η·(∂L/∂w + 2λ·w)` |
| **Effect** | Shrinks weights but rarely to zero |
| **Geometric Shape** | Circle/Sphere (L2 ball) |

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>L1 Regularization (Lasso)</b> — Try to recall the formula first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Penalty** | `λ · Σ |w|` |
| **Gradient Update** | `w ← w - η·(∂L/∂w + λ·sign(w))` |
| **Effect** | Pushes weights **exactly to zero** |
| **Geometric Shape** | Diamond (L1 ball) |
| **Feature Selection** | ✅ Yes |

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Dropout</b> — Try to recall the mechanism first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Training** | Randomly set fraction `p` of neurons to 0 |
| **Inference** | All neurons active; scale outputs by `(1-p)` |
| **Ensemble** | Approximates `2^N` subnetworks with shared weights |
| **Default Rate** | `p = 0.5` (hidden layers) |

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Batch Normalization</b> — Try to recall the operation first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Normalization** | `x̂ = (x - μ_B) / √(σ_B² + ε)` |
| **Learnable Parameters** | Scale `γ`, Shift `β` |
| **Output** | `y = γ·x̂ + β` |
| **Regularization** | Mini-batch noise acts as mild regularizer |

</details>

---

<details>
<summary><b>Layer Normalization</b> — Try to recall the operation first</summary>

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Normalization** | Across features per sample (not batch) |
| **Best For** | RNNs, Transformers, variable-length sequences |
| **Regularization** | Enforces structural stability |

</details>
